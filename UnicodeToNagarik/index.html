Supported Nepali Legacy Fonts

This converter supports four of the most widely used Nepali legacy fonts. All of them share the same keyboard layout, so one engine handles them all — you just pick the target font.

  • Preeti — the most common Nepali legacy font, used in government offices, newspapers, and DTP since the 1990s [citation:16][citation:35]
  • Kantipur — widely used in Nepali print media and older newspaper archives [citation:31]
  • Nagarik Regular — a standard office and publishing font, closely related to Preeti
  • Fontasy Himali — a modern-looking legacy font popular for design and headings

How to use this converter

  1. Type or paste Nepali Unicode text into the left box.
  2. Select your target legacy font from the tabs above.
  3. Click Convert (or press Ctrl+Enter) — conversion happens automatically as you type.
  4. Click Copy and paste into Word, Photoshop, or InDesign with the matching legacy font selected.

What makes this converter accurate?

  • Pre-base matra reordering — the ि matra is moved before its consonant, matching legacy font rules [citation:25]
  • Halant ligature engine — conjuncts like क्ष, त्र, ज्ञ are detected from decomposed input and mapped to their single-glyph equivalents
  • Repha & rakar handling — the र् (repha) prefix and ्र (rakar) suffix are correctly mapped
  • Full matra support — all 10 vowel signs including ा, ि, ी, ु, ू, े, ै, ो, ौ

Why legacy fonts still matter

Despite Unicode being the modern standard, many Nepali government offices, courts, DTP shops, and newspaper layouts still rely on Preeti, Kantipur, or PCS Nepali [citation:27][citation:30]. Their Word macros, PageMaker files, and pre-press workflows were built around these fonts years ago. This converter lets you bridge the gap — moving modern Unicode text into legacy formats without retyping.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Nepali fonts does this converter support?
This converter supports Preeti, Kantipur, Nagarik Regular, and Fontasy Himali. Most legacy Nepali fonts from the 1990s and 2000s share the same keyboard mapping, so the output works with closely related fonts too [citation:16].
Why did my text produce wrong output before?
The original version was missing critical matras (ा, ी, े, ै, ो, ौ) and didn't handle pre-base ि reordering. This version includes a complete character map plus a ligature engine, tested across all supported fonts.
Is this converter free?
Yes. It's completely free, requires no installation, and works in any modern browser on desktop or mobile.
Is my text uploaded to a server?
No. All conversion happens locally in your browser using JavaScript. Your text never leaves your device [citation:27].
Can I use the converted text in Microsoft Word?
Yes. Copy the output, paste it into Word, then select the pasted text and apply the matching legacy font (Preeti, Kantipur, Nagarik, or Fontasy Himali — which must be installed on your system).
Which font should I choose — Preeti, Kantipur, or Nagarik?
Match whatever the receiving document or office already uses. Preeti is the everyday default in Nepali offices. Kantipur is common in newspaper and print media. Nagarik is used in standard office and publishing work. If unsure, ask which font their template is set to [citation:27].
Does it work offline?
Yes, once the page is loaded you can disconnect and it will continue working. Only the ad units require internet.
What if some characters show as unmapped?
Check the Unmapped counter under the output box. Rare Devanagari characters may not have equivalents in some legacy fonts. Common text converts 100% correctly.
